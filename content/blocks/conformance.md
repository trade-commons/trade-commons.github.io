A register entry is worth something because of what had to happen before it. Anyone can assert that
their system is interoperable. An entry in a UN register says that somebody built it, connected it to
real data, ran the published tests against it, and, where the register asks for one, obtained an
independent assessment. The entry is the visible part; the sequence behind it is what a reader of the
register is actually relying on.

The Commons exists to make that sequence cheap. It supplies the components so that building is not a
prerequisite to participating, the test suites so that checking your own work costs nothing, and the
assessment service so that an attested result is available to anyone who needs one. None of it is
compulsory. A vendor that writes its own implementation, tests it against the open suites and
declares its own conformity has done the same thing, and the register does not record whose code was
used.

**The registers belong to UN/CEFACT and not to the Commons.** They are public goods residing on
UNECE sites, and being recorded on one never depends on going through the Commons. The terms on
which the Commons may populate or operate a register are set out under
[governance](/governance).
