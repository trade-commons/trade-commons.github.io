International trade runs to roughly 25 trillion United States dollars of annual exports, moving
between some ten million businesses as two billion consignments through a hundred thousand ports. The
cost, fraud and friction carried by the paper documentation underneath it is proportionate, and that
is the opportunity the cases below quantify.

**The Commons does not make this case and does not own it.** UN/CEFACT maintains the business case
for UNTP and for UNVTD, each with its own methodology and cited evidence. This page points at that
work and does not restate its figures, because UNVTD in particular keeps every number in exactly one
place so the published cases cannot drift from the evidence behind them. A second copy here would be
a second thing to keep true.

What the Commons changes is the other side of the arithmetic. Every one of these cases nets a benefit
against a cost of implementation, and that cost is what the components, the open test suites and the
conformity testing exist to reduce. A case that pays back in four years at one implementation cost
pays back sooner at a lower one, and for the smallest participants the difference decides whether it
pays back at all. That is the whole argument for funding shared infrastructure instead of letting
each adopter build its own.

Read the methodology before quoting any figure. The conservatism and attribution assumptions are
deliberate, and a number lifted out of them says something different from what its authors meant.
